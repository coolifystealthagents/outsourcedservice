A prospective buyer's request to speak with a customer sounds like scheduling work, but the exchange can create privacy, relationship, and advertising risk. A customer may have consented to one private call, not repeated calls, a published quotation, logo use, or a claim about results.

A Philippines-based administrative specialist can qualify the request, check recorded consent, protect the customer's time, arrange logistics, and preserve approved materials. Account owners still select customers, approve representations and incentives, grant logo or publicity rights, and decide whether a reference is appropriate.

## Qualify the requester and purpose

Record the prospect organization, named participants, sales owner, opportunity context, requested format, topics, intended audience, and whether anything will be recorded or published. “Reference request” is too broad to match against consent.

Confirm the request through the company's relationship owner. Do not send customer details merely because an external person claims to be evaluating a purchase. Use approved contact channels and disclose only what is necessary for coordination.

If the requester wants performance figures, regulated information, security details, or confidential workflow, flag those topics before a customer is approached. A reference call should not become an uncontrolled diligence session.

## Match consent to this use

The record should show who consented, on behalf of which organization, approved audience and medium, permitted claims or materials, start and expiry, frequency limits, and revocation route. Silence, an old friendly email, or previous participation should not be stretched into permanent permission.

Private call consent does not imply public endorsement. Logo use does not imply permission to quote an individual. Approval for a named case study does not automatically cover a new industry campaign. Route gaps to the account and legal owners.

FTC guidance concerning endorsements, reviews, and testimonials is relevant to public marketing claims and disclosures. Qualified owners should translate applicable requirements into policy. The coordinator follows the recorded permission and approved language rather than deciding whether a use is legally acceptable.

## Protect customer burden

Track recent requests, completed calls, declines, and pause periods. A satisfied customer can become dissatisfied if several salespeople contact them independently. Use one relationship owner and a single queue.

Offer the customer a clear choice of time and format, explain the requester and purpose, and make declining easy. Do not imply that participation affects service. If an incentive exists, its approval and disclosure treatment belong with authorized owners and should be settled before outreach.

Set a preparation limit for the prospect and a duration for the call. Send agenda topics that the customer and account owner can review. Questions outside the approved scope can be declined or routed later.

## Use a controlled introduction

The coordinator can draft the introduction from approved facts: names, roles, organizations, meeting purpose, scheduled time, and contact route. Avoid claims such as “our most successful customer” or “they reduced costs by 40%” unless those exact representations are substantiated and approved for this use.

Share contact information only with consent and through the approved channel. A calendar invitation may expose attendee addresses or titles. Check visibility settings and avoid attaching account records.

FTC data-security guidance recommends limiting information and access. A coordination case generally needs consent scope and logistics, not the customer's contracts, pricing, or complete account history.

## Preserve what was approved

Keep the request, consent evidence, approved introduction, agenda, attendance, and any follow-up use. Do not record the meeting unless all required permissions and policies are satisfied. If notes are allowed, label who created them and prevent informal notes from becoming approved testimonial copy.

When a prospect later asks to publish a quote from the call, open a new approval step. Provide the exact proposed wording, placement, audience, duration, and attribution. The customer and authorized company owners should approve the use before publication.

If a customer revokes consent, update the controlled register and stop future scheduling. Route questions about existing published materials to the appropriate owner rather than promising immediate removal or arguing about prior permission.

## Audit representations and fairness

Sample cases from request to completion. Confirm requester identity, current consent, frequency rule, approved introduction, secure sharing, and follow-up use. Check that coordinators did not invent performance claims or pressure customers to participate.

Useful measures include requests with complete qualification, consent gaps caught before outreach, customer requests within frequency limits, declines honored, meeting logistics completed, and public-use requests routed for separate approval. A raw number of completed references can reward overuse of the same customers.

Review the customer pool for concentration. Relationship owners may need to broaden or reduce the program; the coordinator reports the pattern without selecting a customer based on protected or inappropriate criteria.

Create scenario cards for a private prospect call, analyst inquiry, recorded event, written quote, logo request, and case study. Each should identify required consent and owner. Records-management discipline keeps the history interpretable, but follow the company's retention schedule and remove unnecessary personal copies.

Start with the existing consent register and the last ten requests. Reconcile what was promised with what occurred. A bounded [administrative support role](/services/admin-support) can make references respectful and orderly while consent, customer selection, claims, incentives, and publicity remain deliberate owner decisions.
