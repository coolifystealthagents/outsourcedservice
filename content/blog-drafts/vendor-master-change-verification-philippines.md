A request to change a supplier's bank details can look like ordinary data maintenance. It is not. A convincing email may be a legitimate update, a compromised mailbox, or an attempt to redirect the next payment. The administrative work can be delegated to a Philippines-based specialist, but the authority to edit the vendor master and release funds should remain elsewhere.

The useful outsourced role is evidence preparation. The specialist opens a controlled case, checks the request against approved records, performs an independent verification step, and gives an authorized owner a complete decision packet. They do not treat a reply from the requesting email address as proof, edit payment fields, approve a supplier, or release a payment.

## Quarantine the request before investigating it

Do not process a bank-change request directly from the inbox. Create a case in a restricted queue and place any affected payment on the organization's documented hold path. The case should capture the vendor identifier, request received time, requested effective date, requester name, affected entities and currencies, and the original message as an attachment or stable link.

Preserve the message without forwarding it widely. A bank form may contain account data, signatures, telephone numbers, and other information that should not appear in general chat. The FTC's business data-security guidance recommends knowing what information the business holds, keeping only what it needs, and limiting access. Those principles make a purpose-built case preferable to an informal email chain.

The intake record should say “change requested,” not “new bank confirmed.” Language matters because queue labels often become implicit approvals. Give the case a unique identifier and record each later action against it.

## Verify through an independent route

The central control is simple: do not use contact information supplied in the change request to verify that same request. Retrieve a previously approved telephone number or relationship contact from a controlled vendor record. If policy allows a callback, use that route. If the stored record is missing or itself under dispute, escalate rather than improvising from a web search.

A verification script should identify the caller, explain the narrow purpose, and avoid reading the proposed account number in full. Ask the established contact to confirm that an authorized change was requested and to follow the organization's approved confirmation process. Record the date, time, route used, person reached, and outcome. A voicemail or unanswered call is an attempt, not confirmation.

Email can carry documents, but email alone should not become independent verification when the mailbox may be the point of compromise. CISA's phishing guidance highlights suspicious requests and encourages verification through trusted channels. Apply the company's own risk policy to decide how many attempts, which channels, and which roles are acceptable.

## Build a comparison without exposing account data

The preparer can compare non-sensitive attributes: legal supplier name, vendor identifier, requesting domain, invoicing entity, currency, and the last approved change date. Account values should be masked according to policy. A report generally needs only enough characters to distinguish the old and proposed destination, not a reusable banking credential.

Create three columns: approved record, requested record, and verification result. Label mismatches rather than resolving them. A new legal name, changed country, unusual effective date, or request made just before a payment run deserves its own exception. So does pressure to bypass the normal callback because an invoice is “urgent.” Urgency changes the escalation time; it does not remove the control.

Do not ask the specialist to judge whether documents are genuine unless the business has a defined authentication method and has trained them to use it. A letterhead, stamp, or signature may support the file, but visual plausibility is not authorization.

## Keep duties separated

The same person should not prepare the evidence, alter the vendor master, and release the payment. GAO's internal-control standards describe segregation of duties as dividing responsibilities so that one person does not control all key aspects of a transaction. NIST security controls likewise provide a framework for separating duties and controlling privileged access.

A workable handoff uses explicit states: received, quarantined, independent contact pending, evidence complete, owner decision pending, approved or rejected, master-data change completed, and post-change review complete. Every transition records the actor and time. Only named approvers may move the case through decision states.

If a small organization cannot fully separate roles, document a compensating review. For example, one authorized owner may approve the evidence and a different authorized user may perform the system change, followed by a report reviewed before payment. The specialist can coordinate those steps without receiving the permissions themselves.

## Route exceptions instead of solving them

Common exceptions need predetermined destinations. A possible mailbox compromise goes to security. A dispute about the vendor's legal entity goes to procurement or legal. A payment already released goes to treasury and the financial institution under the incident procedure. A request involving sanctions, tax, or regulatory questions goes to the qualified owner.

The specialist's exception note should be factual: “Callback to stored number reached a person who denied requesting the change.” It should not declare fraud or accuse an individual. Preserve the evidence and stop the normal workflow.

Set service clocks for follow-up and escalation. Cases should not disappear because verification failed. The dashboard can show age, next action, assigned owner, payment-hold state, and unresolved exception type without displaying full banking details.

## Give the approver a reproducible packet

The final packet should include the original request, the prior approved contact source, a log of independent verification attempts, masked old and proposed values, discrepancies, relevant supporting records, and the exact decision requested. It should also state what the preparer did not verify.

Before handoff, check that no requested contact detail was used as the independent route, all sensitive values are appropriately masked, every evidence item has a source, and no system change or approval was performed by the preparer. The approver should be able to reproduce the checks without asking the specialist to remember an off-system conversation.

Measure the preparation lane through cases with complete evidence, attempts made within the control window, exceptions routed correctly, and packets returned for missing information. Do not reward raw speed if it encourages weak callbacks or skipped holds.

A bounded [accounting and bookkeeping support role](/services/accounting-and-bookkeeping) can make vendor-change cases orderly while preserving the control that matters: verification and preparation are not authorization. Start with the organization's vendor policy, name each decision owner, and test the route with both a normal change and a suspicious request before granting broader access.
